## What changes for players

- **Renown.** A hull's renown is won by sinking another player's hull: the sunk hull's weight (a boat 25, a ship 200, a warship 285), split among the hull that sank her and her grouped allies in sight. The loser drops it, and her crew lose a further 1% of their experience per 30 renown lost. NPC kills train crews but move no renown. Renown survives the wreck registry and trade-ins. `shipcrew` shows it, and `shiprenown` lists the ten most renowned hulls.
- **Prize money.** Every sinking by a player's hull pays the same sharers salvage (an eighth of the sunk hull's value scaled by what is left of her, plus half the price of each surviving weapon), a bounty of 2.5 gold per renown point above 100 (raiders now carry 150-3,000 renown), and the whole WANTED or HUNTED bounty of an owner who went down aboard, which is then cleared. It is paid through the existing settlement queue with a mail receipt.
- **Renown gates.** Able hands need 540-700 renown and veteran hands 1,350-1,640. A capital weapon needs 1,600-2,000 renown, or a veteran gunner aboard as before. Raiders scale with the quarry's renown.
- **Ship Damage Control**, an epic feat of five ranks, cuts every blow to the hull and sails of a vessel its holder owns and is aboard by 4% plus 4% per rank.
- **Contraband.** Forbidden tomes, rare poisons, and dragon eggs are each sold at one sea port, to a hull of 150, 200, or 250 renown or one with an able sailmaster and quartermaster, never to a warship or a buyer at alignment 1,000. Other ports pay their scarce price. Lawful ports' customs search hulls sailing in and seize contraband they do not stock, at Duris's odds: more likely with more units, less with renown, and much less in a full hold.
- **Cargo sales.** A seller with the SEADOG feat gets +10%, a hull under neutral colors -10%, and a warship -40%.

[P2] Make sinking rewards durable and idempotent as one settlement

If a player's hull was saved during its sinking countdown and the process stops after this payout loop but before `vessel_wreck_hull()` saves the wreck, the victor's added renown and prize claims survive while the victim reloads with its old countdown, renown, and `last_attacker`. It sinks again and awards the same renown and salvage/renown bounty a second time. The existing insurance path explicitly handles this recovery case with a marker, but `vessel_pay_prize()` uses `loss = FALSE` and the new reward path records no completed sinking. There is also an irreversible loss window because the owner's bounty is cleared at line 124 before any prize claim commits. Persist a unique sinking settlement and atomically record its claims, bounty consumption, and renown changes, so recovery can finish the wreck without issuing another reward.

Reviewed at `d4f5993c88ce1a8bf3f5d3c2d4e10783e51d839b`.

[P2] Keep contraband sale prices consistent across batch sizes

At a port without a stock row, this always returns `TRADE_SUPPLY_MIN`, and `port_adjust_supply()` updates no row. However, `do_cargosell()` still calls `vessel_trade_sell_revenue()`, which increases a simulated supply after every unit within a batch. With no sale modifiers, selling 100 forbidden tomes together pays 19,942 gold; selling the same tomes one at a time pays 24,800 gold because each command starts at supply 10 again. This lets command splitting bypass the price reduction and penalizes the normal `cargosell ... all` workflow. Price every unit at the fixed scarce rate when the port does not stock the contraband, or persist demand separately from whether the port sells it, so splitting a shipment cannot change its proceeds.

[P2] Distinguish failed stock lookups from unstocked contraband

`port_stocks()` returns `FALSE` both for a missing row and for connection, prepare, execute, or fetch failures. `vessel_customs_inspection()` interprets every such result as permission to confiscate. Thus a ship returning contraband to the very port that stocks it can lose its cargo during a database failure; with 140 units and zero renown the inspection takes all units, even though that cargo should be exempt. The manifest is changed in memory before its save, so a later successful save can make the wrongful seizure permanent. Return an explicit lookup-error outcome and defer the inspection without changing cargo unless the absence of the port's stock row was successfully established.

Reviewed at `d4f5993c88ce1a8bf3f5d3c2d4e10783e51d839b`.
