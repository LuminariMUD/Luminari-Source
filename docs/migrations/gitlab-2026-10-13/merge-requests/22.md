## Description

Closes #14

S15 of the vessel study (`docs/ongoing-projects/vessels-ships.md`, Phase 15), the last step: the
vessel commands that take or pay gold now write and check both stores inside the command, the
captain's gold in the player file and the ship's side in MariaDB. Before, the ship's side was
written at once and the gold moved in memory only, so a crash before the next per-minute save
kept a purchase and gave its gold back, or took a sold item and never paid.

Review range: `vessels-s15-base..vessels-s15` (the S14 close-out `16c6611fb` comes first; the
plan is `c295d6aa8`, the build `aec826b19`, the gate step `6a1ada514` and `77cc74137`, the
fixes found before hand-off `bc2a45b69`). Review round 1 is on top, `vessels-s15..feat/vessels-s15`:
see the last section.

## What changes

- **A purchase takes the gold first.** `vessel_charge()` checks that the database answers, saves
  the debit with `save_char_checked()` and refuses the purchase when either fails, before
  anything aboard has changed. If the ship's side then cannot be written, it is put back and the
  gold is returned (`vessel_refund()`). If the put-back cannot be written either, the purchase
  stands and stays paid (review round 1).
- **A payout writes the ship's side first**, then pays and saves. A payment that cannot be saved
  takes the gold back and puts the item back, in memory and in the rows. If the put-back cannot
  be written, the sale stands and the gold is paid in memory ("undone" is never said of a sale
  the rows still hold).
- **Seven shipyard jobs** (`shiphire`, `shipweapon buy`, `shipequip buy`, `shiprearm`, dock
  `shiprepair`, `shipupgrade`, `shipchristen`) copy the hull before they change her;
  `vessel_purchase_recorded()` writes her with the new `vessel_save_hull()` (interior row,
  runtime state, weapons, owner, crew, refits, each checked) and on a failure restores the copy,
  writes her back and refunds. `shipweapon sell` and `shipequip sell` use
  `vessel_sale_recorded()`.
- **`shipbuy`** charges, then spawns; a failed spawn (it rolls itself back) refunds. The rollback
  deletes her rows before it takes her out of play; when they cannot be deleted she stays in
  play and stays paid (review round 1). The spawn writes the runtime row last, so a crash in
  mid-spawn leaves rows that hold the slot at boot, not a hull. **A trade-in** charges what is owed before the fittings are
  counted, rebuilds and saves, then pays for the fittings; a hull that cannot be rebuilt or
  recorded, or a payment that cannot be saved, undoes the rebuild (`vessel_trade_in_undo()`: the
  new interior reclaimed, the hull restored from the copy, her old rooms recreated as boot
  recreates them). An undo that cannot be recorded keeps the charge for the staff to settle
  (review round 1).
- **`shipsummon`** saves the summons (the emptied manifest, then the hull as stowed, cast off
  and with her autopilot stopped) before she leaves the world or anything alongside or aboard
  is touched, so one that cannot be saved leaves her as she was; one that cannot be written back
  either stands (review round 1).
- **`bounty pay`, `marque`**: charge, then the row; when the write reports a failure the row is
  read back, and the fee is returned only if the row does not show the write.
- **`contractdeliver`**: the contract and the manifest in one transaction (sent once more after
  a `COMMIT` without a reply; both writes are absolute), then the payout. When neither the
  delivery nor taking it back can be confirmed, the captain and the staff are told.
- `shipupgrade` now saves the runtime row with the refit bit: the raised ceilings were in memory
  only until the next runtime save, so a crash kept "installed" without its points.

New code is in `src/vessels/vessels_payment.c`; the commands change in their own files.

## Where to look

- Outside a transaction, a statement sent on a connection that has just been lost does not fail:
  the library reconnects and sends it again. A single statement fails only with its reply lost,
  after the server ran it. That is why a refused purchase is **written back**, not only restored
  in memory, and why the admiralty's rows are read back.
- The undo of the shipyard jobs is `*ship = *before`. Nothing outside the struct changes between
  the copy and the restore at those seven sites; `shipchristen` renames the hull object and the
  rooms only after the write.
- `vessel_db_save_runtime()` saves a stowed hull's own location, not the room of a hull object
  that still stands (the summons is saved while it does).
- `vessel_save_one()` now fails when the owner, crew or refit write fails; it still attempts
  every part past the interior, runtime and weapon rows. `vessel_save_hull()` stops at the first
  failure.
- Two faults, left as they are and recorded: the bounty pay-off and `marque` return the fee when
  the write reports a failure and the row cannot be read back either.
- A database that does not answer refuses these purchases before the gold is taken; the tests
  that bought and sold in memory only now use the test database.
- Not S14's settlement record, as planned: a crash between a command's two writes still splits
  the stores, always against the player (paid and not delivered, or delivered and not paid).

**[P2] A summons puts the carts aboard ashore with the shipyard's coordinates**

`vehicle_release_all_from_vessel()` copies the hull's position into each vehicle it releases (`vehicle->x_coord = (int)vessel->x`, `src/vessels/vehicles_transport.c:602`). Before this MR the summons released the vehicles first and overwrote `ship->x` / `ship->y` afterwards. The new order saves the summons first, so by this line `ship->x` / `ship->y` already hold the summoning shipyard's coordinates (set at lines 568-569). The cart is left in the room where the hull was, carrying, in memory and in its `vehicle_data` row, the coordinates of the shipyard she was called to.

A vehicle's coordinates are its position: `drive` moves it to `x_coord + dx, y_coord + dy` and carries every rider there (`src/vessels/vehicles.c:1739`, `src/vessels/vehicles_commands.c:602-613`), and boot places it by coordinates alone (`vehicle_restore_runtime_location()`).

**Repro** (probe test on `6c0975cd1` against the test database, the `loss_harbor` fixture of `test_vessel_loss.c`: hull at sea in room 1 at (10,12), captain on the dock at (40,41), one cart with `parent_vessel_id` = the hull):

```

cart after summons: parent=0 location(rnum)=1 x=40 y=41
vehicle_data row:   x_coord=40 y_coord=41
```

In play: load a cart aboard a hull lying at A, have a friend wait aboard, and `shipsummon` her from shipyard B. The friend is put over the side at A beside the cart; they mount it, `drive north`, and stand next to B at once with whatever the cart carries, however long the hull's passage is. ```

**[P3] A refunded `shipbuy` can still leave a hull that the next boot rebuilds for the buyer**

`do_shipbuy` refunds whenever the spawn returns -1 (`vessels_edit.c:1163-1168`), and the spawn's rollback does not look at whether `vessel_delete_persistence(slot)` committed (`vessels_edit.c:808`). The new write order covers three of the four writes: rows left without a runtime row hold the slot at boot. It does not cover the runtime row itself.

**Case**: the `REPLACE INTO ship_runtime_state` reaches the server and is applied, and its reply is lost (the case this MR writes back or reads back at every other site). `vessel_db_save_runtime()` returns FALSE. If the connection that lost the reply is the start of an outage (a network cut between the game and the database outlasting the read timeout, a database restart), the rollback's `START TRANSACTION` cannot reconnect and `vessel_delete_persistence()` returns FALSE. The buyer reads "The ship could not be persisted, so the spawn was rolled back. Your N gold is returned.", and the refund is saved to the player file.

The database now holds her whole record: interior row, weapons, owner, runtime row. Unlike the shipyard jobs, no hull is left in memory, so no later save writes over those rows and nothing converges ("held" only keeps the slot from being reused). At the next boot `load_all_ship_interiors()` finds an interior row with a runtime snapshot and rebuilds her, owned by the buyer, who has the price back.

**Repro** (by inspection: no unit world generates interiors): `mysql_test_drop_connection_at("REPLACE INTO ship_runtime_state", 1, TRUE)` inside `vessel_spawn_from_prototype()` with the database out of reach for the delete that follows; then count the slot's rows in `ship_runtime_state` and read the purse.

**Fix**: do not undo a spawn whose rows cannot be removed. Try `vessel_delete_persistence(slot)` before the hull is torn down; when it does not commit, keep her in play and return the slot, so the purchase stands, the fee stays paid, and her next save writes her rows (there is no periodic fleet save: see the thread on `vessel_purchase_recorded()`), as `vessel_sale_recorded()` lets a sale stand when its put-back cannot be written. Every outcome is then a paid hull, or a paid buyer without one after a crash (the direction the MR accepts), never a free hull.

**[P3] A purchase whose write-back fails is called undone and refunded while her rows keep it**

When the hull cannot be written and the write-back fails too, `vessel_purchase_recorded()` logs a SYSERR, refunds, and tells the buyer "nothing was done and your N gold is returned". `vessel_sale_recorded()` refuses to do that for a sale ("Undone" is never said of a sale her rows still hold); the purchase side, the summons (`vessels_loss.c:591-602`) and the trade-in undo (`vessels_edit.c:951-956`, refund at `:1053`) all do it.

The two failures are one event, not two: a reply lost on a statement the server ran is usually the start of an outage, and the write-back's first statement then cannot reconnect.

**Repro** (probe test on `6c0975cd1` against the test database, the `payment_berth` fixture of `test_vessel_payment.c`):

```c
payment_command(&berth, do_shiphire, "gunner green");

```

```

memory: gunner tier=0  gold=200000 (was 200000)
file:   gold=200000
rows:   SELECT COUNT(*) FROM ship_crew_roster WHERE ship_id = 487  ->  1
```

The log's "until she is saved again" is open-ended: there is no periodic fleet save. `save_all_vessels()` runs at copyover (`act.wizard.c:5278`) and after the game loop ends (`comm.c:862`), and her crew and refit rows are otherwise rewritten only by her next shipyard job, dismissal or crew change. A crash or a kill before one of those boots her with the gunner (`vessel_db_load_crew()`), and the 2,400 gold is back in the player file. **Fix**: refund, and say "nothing was done", only when the write-back succeeded. When it fails, let the purchase stand, as the sale does: keep a copy of the hull as bought, put it back in memory, keep the fee (it is already saved), and log; her next save completes the rows. A summons that cannot be written back carries on as summoned. The trade-in has already rebuilt the old hull when its write-back fails: keep the charge there and tell the staff with `mudlog()`, as `contractdeliver` does. So that a database that is away before the command still refuses the purchase without charging, check that it answers before `vessel_charge()`.
